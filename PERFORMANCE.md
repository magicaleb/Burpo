# Echo Trace — performer notes

## What the participant experiences

1. On their own phone, they open a deliberately bogus doodle personality test.
2. They are clearly told the drawing remains on their device and that the screen should stay private.
3. They draw one free, continuous symbol and seal it.
4. Their drawing vanishes. A dry, tongue-in-cheek personality verdict appears and they read it aloud.
5. They place their phone face down. You have not touched it and do not need your own phone.
6. You draw an impression on paper and commit it to the table.
7. They turn their phone over, reveal their original trace, and make the comparison themselves.

The participant’s phone never displays a magical change or a forced selection. The effect is the
agreement between a private digital action and a physical drawing made elsewhere.

## The memory map

The engine smooths hand wobble, finds actual corners, and reduces the participant’s continuous
stroke to two through seven important regions on a 3×3 map. One concrete noun in the verdict
represents each region, in route order.

| Position | Anchor | Example words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top-left | Cloud | mist, rain, thunder, breeze, frost, storm |
| Top-center | Sun | sunlight, lantern, glow, dawn, shadow, candle |
| Top-right | Star | moon, comet, star, orbit, planet, eclipse |
| Middle-left | Tree | pine, cedar, fern, garden, willow, orchard |
| Center | Heart | memory, dream, thought, heartbeat, secret, echo |
| Middle-right | Bird | sparrow, robin, raven, swallow, magpie, pigeon |
| Bottom-left | Stone | pebble, crystal, marble, flint, fossil, gemstone |
| Bottom-center | Wave | river, tide, lake, brook, ocean, harbor |
| Bottom-right | Flame | ember, flame, spark, fire, cinder, torch |

The mnemonic structure is spatial:

- Above you: weather, light, space.
- Around you: plants, mind, birds.
- Below you: stone, water, fire.

Do not memorize all 54 words individually. Learn to recognize the nine semantic families.

## Suggested script

> This is a highly scientific personality test, provided we use a very relaxed definition of both
> “scientific” and “test.” Draw one simple thing in a single line and keep the screen to yourself.

When the line appears:

> Read the verdict aloud. Apparently the algorithm becomes more accurate when publicly challenged.

As they read, translate each image noun into a point on the map. Connect the points mentally as one
continuous stroke. Ask them to place the phone face down before you begin drawing.

Commit the broad geometry first. If it suggests a familiar object or letter, name that impression
only after the skeleton is on paper. Set your pen down before they touch their phone again.

> For the first time, turn the phone over and compare what you drew with what I committed to.

## Practical boundaries

Echo Trace is strongest with a continuous line that uses the canvas: an arrow, lightning
bolt, crown, mountain, simple face profile, letterform, or abstract zigzag. The participant UI asks
for this without displaying a suspicious grid or limiting them to named options.

The routine is a structural drawing duplication, not a pixel-for-pixel impression device. A loop,
curve, or tiny flourish may not survive route compression. Reveal the overall movement confidently;
let the participant supply the emotional weight of the match.

## Rehearsal progression

1. Learn the nine anchors from `studio.html`.
2. Decode short verdicts until you can visualize the line before the sentence ends.
3. Add five- through seven-node routes.
4. Practice redrawing the route as a fluid gesture rather than nine disconnected dots.
5. Rehearse an out: describe the opening direction and broad geometry before drawing when a phrase
   is missed.

## Reset and offline use

After the original is shown, the participant taps **Erase this trace**. Refresh also destroys the
session. Once loaded successfully, participant mode is cached for offline use; rehearse the exact
hosting and home-screen setup before a paid performance.
